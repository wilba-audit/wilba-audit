## 1. What the guru playbook actually teaches (faceless channels, clipping, "repurpose other creators", AI remix)

### Takeaway
The playbook comes in two versions. The "safe" version, which tool vendors now push, copies a successful video's structure or topic, then makes new footage with AI tools. The "risky" version reuploads, clips or lightly edits other creators' footage. Income claims are promotional and unverified. Even vendor and SEO blogs now warn that the reupload version is what YouTube's reused-content and inauthentic-content rules target.

## 2. YouTube policy: reused content, inauthentic content, Content ID/strikes, Shorts, AI disclosure, gaming violence

### Takeaway
YouTube has three separate tracks, each with its own risk:
- **Monetisation (YPP):** the "reused content" and "inauthentic content" rules apply at channel level. Unoriginal content can cost you monetisation on the *whole channel*.
- **Copyright:** Content ID claims, and DMCA takedowns that become strikes. Three active strikes means the channel is terminated.
- **Recommendations:** an October 2026 Shorts update cuts the reach of re-uploads that add nothing.

Disclosure of realistic AI content is mandatory. A September 2026 ad-guideline update governs graphic game violence, which matters directly for GTA.

### Cited Findings

**Reused content (YPP)**
- **[OFFICIAL, via search excerpt]** The YouTube Shorts monetisation policy names as non-original: "unedited clips from others' films or TV shows," "reuploading other creators' content from YouTube or another platform," and "compilations with no original content added." It lists "promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)" as non-monetisable. It says "other policies, such as copyright, still apply," and: "If you have videos that violate our guidelines, or if we cannot clearly tell that you made the content, monetization may be removed from your entire channel." Its examples of *allowed* content include clips used for a critical review, and edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en); [YouTube Help: YPP channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) (excerpts via [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/))
- **[PRESS]** YouTube said in July 2025 that its *reused content* policy, which covers commentary, clips, compilations and reaction videos, was **not** changed. — [TechCrunch, 9 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows) (search summary)

**Inauthentic content (renamed July 2025, re-clarified July 2026)**
- **[PRESS]** In July 2025 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" YPP rule to "inauthentic content," effective 15 July 2025. YouTube described this as a minor update and clarification aimed at mass-produced and repetitive content, not a ban on AI. The rule applies to whole channels. — [TechCrunch, 9 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [AlternativeTo, Jul 2025](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- **[COMMENTARY]** The distinction is that reused content = copying others' material without meaningful transformation, while inauthentic = original-looking but mass-produced or templated content with little human creative input. A channel can break one rule without breaking the other. — [Flocker](https://flocker.tv/posts/youtube-inauthentic-content-ai-enforcement/) (secondary)
- **[PRESS]** On 13 July 2026, Tubefilter reported that YouTube reworked its guidance under the inauthentic content policy, breaking out three specific categories of video that will get channels demonetised. Tubefilter stresses this is "not a new policy." **I could not fetch the article to confirm the three categories.** — [Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); see also [TheDesk, Jul 2026](https://thedesk.net/2026/07/youtube-clamps-down-low-quality-ai-slop/); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/youtube-cracks-down-on-off-putting-content-and-ai-slop-2000787956)

**Shorts: re-uploads and remix**
- **[OFFICIAL quote via press, Oct 2026]** YouTube is "updating our recommendation systems to further prioritize original content and reduce the reach of content re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own." This is a *reach* change, not a monetisation change. Shorts cut from your *own* video are your content and are not targeted. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/phones/youtube-cracks-down-on-reposted-shorts-with-a-new-recommendation-update/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-original-content-update/)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Shorts' built-in Remix feature lets creators sample other videos' audio and clips. One source says YouTube released an AI-powered remix tool in March 2026 but has not clarified whether remix outputs survive the October 2026 originality change. — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/youtube-downranks-re-uploaded-shorts-ai-remix-status-unclear); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/)

**Copyright: Content ID, claims vs strikes, the 3-strike rule**
- **[OFFICIAL/REFERENCE]** If a user gets three copyright strikes within 90 days, YouTube terminates the channel and any associated channels, removes all its videos, and bars the user from creating new channels. If you complete Copyright School and have fewer than three strikes, each strike expires 90 days after it was issued. — [Wikipedia: YouTube copyright strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_copyright_strike) (summarising YouTube Help); [TrademarkEngine, 2026](https://www.trademarkengine.com/blog/youtube-copyright-claims-vs-copyright-strikes-creators-need-to-know/)
- **[COMMENTARY]** A Content ID *claim* mainly redirects or blocks revenue on that one video. - **[OFFICIAL, via search excerpt]** From YouTube's "Fair use on YouTube" page: "Giving credit to the owner of a copyrighted work won't by itself turn a non-transformative copy of their material into fair use." Transformativeness is usually key. — [YouTube Help: Fair use on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en); [YouTube Help: Fair use FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6396261)

**Altered/synthetic (AI) content disclosure**
- **[OFFICIAL]** Since March 2024, creators must disclose realistic content that is meaningfully altered or synthetically generated (including with generative AI) when a viewer could mistake it for real. Obviously unrealistic, animated, or minor edits (e.g. beauty filters) are exempt. Disclosure happens in the upload flow and adds an "Altered or synthetic content" label. YouTube may apply a label itself, which the creator can't remove, where the content could mislead. Extra prominence applies to sensitive topics, including finance. — [YouTube Help: Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [YouTube Official Blog: How we're helping creators disclose altered or synthetic content](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); [9to5Google, 18 Mar 2024](https://9to5google.com/2024/03/18/youtube-altered-content-disclosure/)

**Graphic gaming violence (Sept 2026), directly relevant to GTA 6**
- **[PRESS]** In early September 2026, ahead of GTA 6's 19 Nov launch, YouTube updated its advertiser-friendly guidelines. Graphic game violence in the opening seconds of a video makes it ineligible for ads. Outlets disagree on the cutoff: some say 7 seconds, others 8. Gaming drug depictions were made ad-eligible. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gta/youtube-updates-gaming-violence-monetization-rules-ahead-of-gta-6-launch-3405945/); [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/youtube-updates-gaming-violence-ad-rules-ahead-of-gta-6); [Northeast Times, 4 Sep 2026](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/09/04/youtube-sets-new-timing-rules-for-ad-revenue-on-graphic-gaming-content/); [Express Tribune, 6 Sep 2026](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2627692/youtube-updates-gaming-violence-monetisation-rules-ahead-of-gta-6-launch); [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/109095/article/youtube-will-not-monetize-clips-with-gratuitous-video-game-violence/)
- **[PRESS]** Separately, a November 2025 policy *age-restricts* gaming content with realistic mass violence against civilians or torture of human-like characters. Age-restricted videos lose monetisation. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/amp/story/gaming/gaming-news/youtube-tightens-age-restrictions-on-violent-gaming-videos)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Base reference: YouTube Help advertiser-friendly content guidelines. — [YouTube Help 9725604](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Taking another creator's GTA footage and running it through AI voice or upscaling tools is still "reuploading other creators' content" with little added. That risks (a) channel-wide YPP removal under reused content, (b) Content ID claims or strikes from the original creator, and (c) Shorts reach suppression.
- AI-generated "realistic" GTA-looking scenes made in Higgsfield, shown as if they were real gameplay proving a code, would likely need the altered/synthetic disclosure. They would also cut against the venture's own "tested, evidence-backed" trust rule.
- For GTA clips: keep the first ~8 seconds and thumbnails free of graphic violence, and blur gore. Treat the exact second count as unconfirmed.

## 3. Fair use basics (US), facts vs expression, and Australia's narrower fair dealing

### Takeaway
A code itself is a fact and nobody owns it. Another creator's footage, voiceover, script, edit and thumbnail *are* copyrighted. US fair use is a four-factor case-by-case test where transformation matters most. Credit and disclaimers don't count as a defence. Jess is in Australia, which has no general fair use. Fair dealing there only covers listed purposes (criticism/review, parody/satire, reporting news, research/study, professional advice) and must also be "fair."

### Cited Findings
- **[LEGAL]** In *Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone* (US Supreme Court, 1991), the Court held facts are not copyrightable. A compilation is protected only in its original selection or arrangement, "not … the facts themselves." — [US Reports 499 U.S. 340 (govinfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-499/pdf/USREPORTS-499-340.pdf); [Harvard Berkman PDF](https://cyber.harvard.edu/people/tfisher/IP/1991_Feist.pdf); [BitLaw](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/cases/copyright/feist.html)
- **[LEGAL]** The four statutory factors (17 U.S.C. §107) are: purpose and character of the use, including whether it is transformative or commercial; nature of the work; amount used; and market effect. No single factor decides the outcome. "Transformative" use (Leval; adopted in *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose*) is central. — [YouTube Help: Fair use on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en); [Cohn Legal Group](https://www.cohnlg.com/fair-use-copyright-law-for-youtube-content-creators/); [Infojustice (Leval/Google Books)](https://infojustice.knowledgegov.org/?p=35175)
- **[OFFICIAL]** "Giving credit to the owner of a copyrighted work won't by itself turn a non-transformative copy of their material into fair use." Fair use is decided by courts, and short clips or disclaimers don't automatically qualify. — [YouTube Help: Fair use on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en); [Wikipedia: YouTube copyright strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_copyright_strike)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Reaction videos can qualify only when they genuinely comment on or criticise the work. Passive watching doesn't. — [Vondran Legal: reaction videos and copyright](https://www.vondranlegal.com/reaction-videos-and-copyright-law-best-practices-to-stay-out-of-court); [VLAA: Three Strikes, You're Out](https://vlaa.org/?p=9802)
- **[AU, OFFICIAL/LAW REFORM]** Australia's Copyright Act 1968 (Cth) has purpose-based fair dealing exceptions: research or study, criticism or review, parody or satire, reporting news, and professional advice. Applying one is a two-step test: the use must be for a listed purpose, and then it must be fair. Criticism/review and news reporting also require "sufficient acknowledgement." — [ALRC: Fair dealing, current law (DP 79)](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/copyright-and-the-digital-economy-dp-79/7-fair-dealing/current-law/); [ALRC Issues Paper 42](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/copyright-and-the-digital-economy-ip-42/fair-dealing-exceptions/)
- **[AU, LAW REFORM]** The ALRC recommended adopting a US-style fair use in Report 122 (2013). My search did not confirm it was ever enacted. Treat Australia as fair dealing only. — [ALRC Report 122](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/copyright-and-the-digital-economy-alrc-report-122/4-the-case-for-fair-use/fair-use-builds-on-australias-fair-dealing-tradition/)
- **[AU, COMMENTARY]** Crediting a creator does not by itself make an infringing use lawful. News reporting needs a real news angle, since entertainment or curation may not qualify. Short excerpts reasonably necessary to review claims can fit criticism/review. — [Sprintlaw: fair use and YouTube in Australia](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/fair-use-youtube/)

### Inferences
- Learning from another creator's video that "code X exists" and then testing it yourself on your own console uses only a *fact*. Your own capture, script and edit are original, so no copyright issue arises from the code itself.
- Using their footage needs a genuine criticism/review or news purpose, used sparingly and transformed. Examples: showing two seconds of a fake-code video while debunking it, with acknowledgement. Even then it is a judgement call, and in Australia it must fit a listed purpose.
- Rockstar/Take-Two's own footage policy is a separate question (game footage is Take-Two's copyright). It is not covered here.

## 4. TikTok and Instagram: unoriginal/reposted content, Creator Rewards, originality ranking, watermarks

### Takeaway
TikTok excludes unoriginal and reposted content from the For You feed and from Creator Rewards payouts. Repeat offences can make a whole account ineligible. Instagram replaces reposted Reels with the original in recommendations. Since 2024 it stops recommending accounts that repost unoriginal content 10+ times in 30 days. In 2026 that was extended platform-wide (photos and carousels too). Visible other-app watermarks are a red flag on both platforms.

### Cited Findings
- **[COMMENTARY citing TikTok rules]** TikTok's Creator Rewards treats "content copied completely from others" and content "reproduced from others with only slight modifications" as unoriginal. Clips qualify only when the edit adds something genuinely new and the video is at least 1 minute. Duets, Stitches and Photo Mode/slideshows are reported as ineligible. Watermarked video (another app's logo) is a strong unoriginality signal. — [FindClout: does TikTok Creator Rewards pay for reposted or clipped content](https://findclout.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-reposted-clips); [ShortsFast: Creator Rewards 2026 requirements](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-2026-requirements/); [Ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/how-to-monetize-clips-with-tiktok-crp)
- **[COMMENTARY, unverified thresholds]** One source says 5 video violations in 30 days makes an account ineligible, and 5 total account violations makes it permanently ineligible. Not confirmed against TikTok docs. — [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-2026-requirements/)
- **[PRESS]** TikTok can make an entire account ineligible for For You recommendations if it repeatedly posts content breaking the For You eligibility standards. Ineligible posts stay up but aren't recommended, and the reason and an appeal option appear in the post's analytics. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/tiktok-is-trying-to-clean-up-its-for-you-recommendations-210057825.html); [Yahoo Tech](https://www.yahoo.com/tech/tiktok-block-users-feed-repeatedly-155549425.html); [TikTok Newsroom, 21 Mar 2023: Community Guidelines update](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/community-guidelines-update)
- **[PRESS]** A May 2026 report says some *original* creators were disqualified from Creator Rewards for "unoriginal content" with little explanation. This is one outlet's account. — [Baller Alert](https://balleralert.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-disqualification/)
- **[OFFICIAL via press]** On 20 Apr 2022, Adam Mosseri announced that Instagram would rank by originality, aimed at aggregator accounts. He said "Identifying 'originality' is hard … we will iterate over time." — [Tubefilter, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/04/21/instagram-adam-mosseri-algorithm-change-original-content/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90744109/instagram-tiktok-algorithm-adam-mosseri)
- **[OFFICIAL via press]** In 2024 Instagram said it would replace reposted Reels with the original in recommendations, add a label crediting the original creator, and stop recommending Reels from accounts that post unoriginal content 10+ times in 30 days. The rule was extended from Reels to the whole platform, including photos and carousels. — [PetaPixel, 30 Apr 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/push-for-original-content-signals-bigger-reset-in-creator-economy-154828.html)

## 5. Real enforcement examples (dated)

### Takeaway
There are documented cases of YouTube demonetising and then terminating large AI or reupload-style channels (2025–2026). Gaming creators have also been hit by strategic DMCA strikes. No case found was specific to a GTA codes channel.

- **[PRESS]** **Screen Culture (India) and KH Studio (Georgia)**, which made fake AI movie trailers splicing official footage with AI images, together had more than 2M subscribers and over 1B views. YouTube suspended their ads after a Deadline investigation (March 2025). Ads were restored after they added "fan trailer"/"parody" labels. In December 2025 both channels were terminated for spam and misleading-metadata violations. Screen Culture reportedly posted 23 fake trailers for *The Fantastic Four: First Steps*, some outranking the real one. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/youtube-terminates-screen-culture-kh-studio-over-ai-fake-movie-trailers/); [heise](https://heise.de/-11121072); [Gigazine, 23 Dec 2025](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251223-youtube-shuts-down-ai-fake-movie-trailers-channels); [Digital Music News, 26 Dec 2025](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/12/26/youtube-ai-film-trailers-takedowns/); [Express Tribune, Jan 2026](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2582980/youtube-terminates-ai-generated-fake-movie-trailer-channels-after-policy-breaches)
- **[PRESS]** A Kapwing study reviewed 15,000 trending channels and found 278 producing only AI slop. ## 6. Using another video as a lead/source vs repurposing its footage

### Takeaway
Treating someone else's video as a *tip* is safe and normal journalism practice: note the claim, cite or link it, then test it yourself and film your own proof. Repurposing their *footage, narration, script or edit* (even AI-rewritten or re-voiced) triggers copyright plus reused/unoriginal-content penalties on all three platforms.

### Cited Findings
- Facts (e.g. a cheat code string, or "Rockstar announced X offer") are not copyrightable. Only original expression is. — [Feist, 499 U.S. 340 (1991)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-499/pdf/USREPORTS-499-340.pdf)
- YouTube flags "reuploading other creators' content" and compilations without original additions as non-original, even with permission. It allows clips used for critical review. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Credit isn't a defence. — [YouTube Help: Fair use on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en); [Sprintlaw (AU)](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/fair-use-youtube/)
- Even Higgsfield's marketing says to copy a proven video's *structure*, not its footage. — [Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/faceless-channel)
- Shorts cut from your own long-form are your content and outside the October 2026 re-upload reach cut. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/)

### Inferences
- Safe workflow for Jess: (1) spot a claimed code in another creator's video or post; (2) log it as "unverified – source: [link, date]"; (3) test on her own console and capture her own footage; (4) publish with her own voice and script, optionally citing "first spotted by @X." That citation is a courtesy and builds trust, but it is not a licence to use their footage.
- If debunking a fake-code video, show a very short excerpt only when needed to criticise it, with acknowledgement. Never use it as filler. In Australia this has to fit "criticism or review" or "reporting news."
- AI tools (Higgsfield/Artlist) fit B-roll, music, graphics and thumbnails. They should not be used to fabricate "proof" gameplay. Doing that would need an AI disclosure and would breach the venture's evidence rule.

### Gaps
- No official platform page spells out "lead vs footage" in those words. This distinction is an inference from copyright law plus the reused-content rules.

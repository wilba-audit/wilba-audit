## 1. Rockstar's current policy on gameplay video, streaming, monetisation, cutscenes, spoilers and leaks (plus GTA 6 takedowns)

### Takeaway
Rockstar's standing policy, "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material" (reported last updated 10 Jan 2025), allows fans to post and stream gameplay for **non-commercial** use. "Non-commercial" is defined as not making money from the footage, so monetised GTA content is *tolerated in practice but not licensed*. Rockstar targets endings, isolated cutscenes, pre-release or leaked footage, and online cheating. Take-Two has repeatedly struck GTA 6 trailer reuploads and reactions that replay the full trailer, leaked footage, and AI fakes. A codes channel built on its own capture, with no leaked material and no full trailer replays, is on the safest footing available, but it is not risk-free if monetised.

### Cited Findings
**The policy itself (via snippets and secondary coverage):**
- Rockstar's support page title is "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material". It defines non-commercial as follows: "'Non-commercial' means that you don't make money through the game footage you post or use the material as part of a promotion for a product or service." — [Rockstar Support (snippet)](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/200153756/Policy-on-posting-copyrighted-Rockstar-Games-material); same wording at [the newer URL](https://support.rockstargames.com/en-US/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz)
- Removal triggers listed in coverage of the policy are: the game's ending, pure cutscenes in isolation, in-game TV shows and performances, pre-release footage (including early unboxings), cheating in online modes, and unauthorised mods or ports. Coverage paraphrases the policy as: "It's ok to show cutscenes as part of a larger play-through (or in a narrated 'Let's Play' type video) but isolated videos of the game's cinematics will be removed." — [Rockstar Support via search summary](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/200153756/rockstar-games-video-policy); [GameSpot, GTA V launch-day coverage, 2013](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/rockstar-outlines-user-upload-policy-on-gtav-launch-day/1100-6414525/); [Game Informer, 16 Sep 2013](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2013/09/16/rockstar-details-allowable-content-and-uses-of-game-footage.aspx)
- Take-Two reserves the right to remove content it finds "objectionable" case by case and to change the policy at any time. The page was reportedly last updated **10 Jan 2025**. Questions go to **copyright@take2games.com**. — [Rockstar Support (snippet summary)](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-material)
- The policy covers all Rockstar games and has no stated end date for its spoiler and ending restrictions. One fan-site guide advises treating the restrictions as applying after launch as well. — [Bloxodes, GTA 6 spoiler video rules (2026, fan site)](https://bloxodes.com/articles/gta-6-spoiler-video-rules)

**Monetisation in practice (secondary sources, opinion):**

**Radio and music (a separate risk from Rockstar's policy):**

### Inferences
- The policy's own wording favours a format built on original capture of the codes being tested, with narration (a "we tested it" format). The things it forbids (isolated cutscenes, endings, pre-release or leaked footage, full trailer replays) are not needed for a codes channel.
- Because monetisation is tolerated rather than licensed, the venture's existing plan to email Take-Two about monetisation (STEP-1 checklist) is sensible. Until then, the channel should avoid sponsor reads placed over gameplay. That is the "promotion for a product or service" clause.
- Muting or turning down in-game radio during capture (audio settings) is a cheap way to avoid Content ID claims that could also mute commentary. **Verify that GTA VI has separate radio and music volume sliders at launch.**
- Early-copy or pre-launch footage would fall under the "pre-release" clause and Take-Two's active leak enforcement. Do not use or reupload it, even as a source.

## 2. How established guide outlets and creators source tips fast, and how they credit

### Takeaway
I found little public, written sourcing policy from the big guide outlets (IGN, GameRant, Dexerto, PowerPyx). What is documented: early review access and publisher review guides give outlets their head start, though not all outlets get access; outlets correct by update or editor's note rather than deletion; and stated norms are to credit the original source and say clearly when a claim can't be corroborated. Reddit and Discord tips are used, but I found no outlet policy that formalises how.

### Cited Findings
- **Head start from early access:** publishers send review codes with a review guide detailing "which aspects of the game shouldn't be revealed in reviews or other derivative works". Embargoes typically lift near launch. — [GameSkinny, "What Exactly Is a Game Embargo?"](https://www.gameskinny.com/culture/what-exactly-is-a-game-embargo/)
- Code timing varies. For example, Avowed review codes went out about 2 weeks before launch, and other outlets got codes only about 2 days before. Access is not universal: "the notion that games writers get access to every single game on the market... is simply not true." — [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/avowed-review-embargo-confirmed-february-13-already-in-reviewers); [Aftermath, Games Journalism FAQ](https://aftermath.site/games-journalism-an-faq)
- **IGN corrections:** IGN generally does not take content down. It uses updates and editor's notes, and states "we don't agree to takedown requests". This comes from a page on an IGN Squarespace domain, not ign.com, so its official status is unconfirmed. — [IGN corrections policy](https://ign-entertainment.squarespace.com/corrections-policy)
- IGN's archived 2015 "Standards and Practices" says the editorial and sales teams work independently. — [Wayback snapshot](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20151106095848/http://www.ign.com/wikis/ign/Standards_and_Practices) (dated)
- **Peer outlets' stated norms:** Insider Gaming says it credits original sources wherever possible. Pure Xbox says "if we are unable to corroborate relevant information, we will always state this clearly in the article", and publishes separate fact-checking and corrections pages. — [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/?p=157440); [Pure Xbox fact-checking](https://www.purexbox.com/fact-checking); [Pure Xbox corrections](https://www.purexbox.com/corrections); [Pure Xbox ethics](https://www.purexbox.com/ethics)
- Other outlets with public editorial policies that could serve as templates: [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/editorial-policy), [Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/site-info/editorial-policy/), [Out of Games](https://outof.games/editorial-standards/), [games.gg](https://games.gg/editorial-standards/), [Push Square coverage policy](https://www.pushsquare.com/coverage)
- **Attribution example:** Windows Central's roundup "clearly credited Insider Gaming for the original reporting". This is cited in an opinion piece as evidence that proper attribution is still possible. — [Indie-Games.eu (opinion)](https://www.indie-games.eu/?p=38212)
- Aftermath has published a guide to reading video game leaks and rumours, useful for a fact-desk standard. — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/video-game-leaks-rumors-guide/)
- Reddit published "pressiquette" guidance (2014) asking journalists to seek permission before covering stories from the site. Critics said it undermined fair use, and its scope was unclear. — [iMediaEthics](https://www.imediaethics.org/do-reddit-new-pressiquette-guidelines-for-journalists-undermine-fair-use/); [The Next Web, 13 Aug 2014](https://thenextweb.com/news/reddit-recommends-journalists-ask-permission-covering-stories-site)

### Inferences
- For a fan site without early access, the documented advantages that remain are: (a) testing claims in-game on launch day yourself, (b) corrections by dated update rather than silent deletion (the IGN model), and (c) explicitly labelling claims as "unconfirmed", "community-reported" or "tested by us" (the Pure Xbox model). This matches the venture's Confirmed / Expired / Unavailable ledger.
- A practical crediting pattern taken from these norms: "Claim first reported by u/X on r/Y (link, date/time). We tested it on PS5 on [date]: result." Asking a Reddit poster for permission before quoting them at length is a courtesy, consistent with Reddit's guidance.

### Gaps
- I found no published sourcing or crediting policy from GameRant, Dexerto or PowerPyx, and no interview with a trophy-guide creator about speed workflow. I also found no current editorial standards page on ign.com itself.
- I found no documented data on how often guide outlets use dataminers, Discord tip lines or Reddit tips.

## 3. Legitimate speed tactics: capture setups, stream-then-clip, templates, AI clipping on your own footage

### Takeaway
PS5 can save up to 60 minutes of recent gameplay from a background buffer and record manually at up to 4K. Pressing the Create button right after a successful code test gives instant, wholly original proof footage. AI clippers (OpusClip, CapCut "Long video to Shorts", Descript) work on your own VODs. Their prices in 2026 sources conflict, so check vendor pages.

### Cited Findings
- **PS5:** you can save from the last 15 seconds up to 1 hour of recent gameplay. Presets are 15s, 30s, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m and 60m. Manual recording goes up to 4K. Settings are under Settings > Captures and Broadcasts > Captures. — [PlayStation Support](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/games/capture-ps5-gameplay-screenshots); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/76220/ps5-can-capture-up-to-1-hour-of-4k-gameplay-video-with-create-button/index.html)

## 4. Reaction and commentary formats that stay monetised, and how to cite a creator's video without reusing footage

### Takeaway
YouTube's own help pages state that there are no "magic words", that giving credit does not grant rights, and (per the US Copyright Office as quoted) that no fixed percentage or number of seconds makes a use fair. The "X% commentary" rule is a myth. Monetisation under the reused-content policy depends on adding significant original commentary or value. The July 2025 rename to "inauthentic content" did not change the reused-content treatment of reactions. For a codes channel, the cleanest way to cite a creator's claim is to link it and credit it on screen, then show your own test footage, never their video.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Help: "there aren't any magic words to automatically apply fair use", and the phrase "no infringement intended" won't protect you. — [YouTube Help, Fair use FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6396261) (snippet)
- YouTube "Common copyright myths": giving credit to the copyright owner "doesn't automatically give you the rights to use their copyrighted content". — [YouTube Help 2797449](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797449) (snippet); the archived YouTube fair-use page lists credit as "Myth #1" and says transformativeness is usually key — [archived youtube.com/yt/copyright/fair-use](https://iza-server.uibk.ac.at/pywb/dilimag/20210113092156mp_/https:/www.youtube.com/yt/copyright/fair-use.html); [YouTube Help, Fair use on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en)
- The US Copyright Office is quoted as saying there are "no legal rules permitting the use of a specific number of words, a certain number of musical notes, or percentage of a work". This is secondhand, via search summaries, and not verified on copyright.gov. — [Adweek guide](https://www.adweek.com/?p=332716); [usethirdchair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/youtube-fair-use-what-actually-counts-in)
- Commercial "2026 guides" claim reactions need ≥40% commentary. There is no clear legal basis for this, so treat it as a myth. — [fluxnote (example)](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-react-video-copyright-2026)
- **Reused content (YPP):** this covers content repurposed without significant original commentary, modifications or value. Reaction videos with commentary or substantive edits can be eligible. Videos that are mostly non-verbal reactions without voice commentary are ineligible. Violations can remove monetisation from the **entire channel**. — [YouTube Help 1311392 (snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/understanding-youtubes-reused-content-policy)
- **July 2025:** YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and stated "There is no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations and reaction videos." The update targets mass-produced or repetitive content. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/never-fear-reaction-videos-are-still-allowed-under-youtubes-new-inauthentic-content-policy-222401009.html); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- **Conflict:** one marketing blog says reaction videos can't be monetised. This contradicts YouTube's statements above. — [lilachbullock.com](https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=188338)
- Content ID does not weigh fair use. A reaction that substitutes for the original weighs against fair use, and courts, not platforms, decide fair use. — [Vondran Legal / JDSupra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/reaction-videos-and-copyright-law-best-9687112/); [Fordham IPLJ, 24 May 2024](https://fordhamiplj.org/2024/05/24/reacting-right-fair-use-and-reaction-content/)
- Law-firm guidance suggests linking or embedding the original rather than reuploading it, and using only as much as the point requires. Credit is good practice but has no legal effect on fair use. — [Vondran Legal](https://www.vondranlegal.com/reaction-videos-and-copyright-law-best-practices-to-stay-out-of-court); [BossWallah, "Credit to Owner will not save your channel"](https://bosswallah.com/blog/creator-hub/youtube-copyright-why-credit-to-owner-will-not-save-your-channel/)
- Trailer reaction videos that replayed the full GTA 6 trailer with audio were claimed by Take-Two (see section 1). — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/gta-6-livestreamers-tiktok-youtube-copyright-strike-1851074512)

### Inferences
- **Recommended citation format for a codes video (opinion, based on the above):**
  1. On screen, a text lower-third: "Claim: [code/effect] — first seen from @Creator (YouTube/TikTok), [date]".
  2. In the description, a direct link to the creator's video or post, plus a timestamp.
  3. Then *your own* footage of entering the code, with your result. No clips of the other creator's video.
  4. A screenshot of a **text** claim (a Reddit or X post title, with the username visible or blurred per policy) is a lower-risk factual citation than a frame of someone's video. It is still copyrighted material, so keep it brief and on screen only as long as needed to identify the source. This is an inference; I found no source specifically on screenshots of text posts.
- "Reaction" formats that suit this venture without reusing footage are "Myth vs Tested" (you read the claim aloud, then show your test), "Viewer-submitted codes tested", and "Scam alert breakdowns" with your own narration. All add original verbal analysis, which is the factor the reused-content policy rewards.
- Avoid compilations of other creators' clips, even when credited. Credit is no defence, and compilations are named in the reused-content review list.

## 5. Search and discovery tactics, plus monitoring tools (free or cheap)

### Takeaway
A mix of free tools can surface new claims within minutes to hours: F5Bot (Reddit keyword email alerts, free, with a delay on the free tier), X advanced search operators (`since:`, `min_faves:`, `-filter:replies`; login required), YouTube's upload-date filter, Google Alerts, and subreddit RSS. Paid listening tools (Syften, Octolens) cover X and YouTube too. Treat all of these as leads only, to be tested before publishing.

### Cited Findings
- **F5Bot** monitors Reddit, Hacker News and Lobsters for keywords and emails "within minutes". It is free with no card needed. On the free plan alerts arrive after a delay, while paid plans send them immediately. It has no regex or wildcards, and RSS/JSON output appears to be paid ("Power"). Free-tier limits are reported inconsistently (200 keywords / 50 alerts a day versus 5 keywords / 20 alerts a day). — [F5Bot](https://f5bot.com/); [F5Bot FAQ](https://f5bot.com/faq); [F5Bot guide](https://f5bot.com/guides/reddit-keyword-alerts); [Redship review](https://redship.io/reddit-tool/f5bot); [getairefs](https://getairefs.com/blog/f5bot-alternatives-2026/)
- F5Bot does not cover X, Facebook or LinkedIn. Paid alternatives include Syften, Octolens and Apify scrapers. — [Redship](https://redship.io/best-f5bot-alternatives.md); [Scrape Creators](https://scrapecreators.com/blog/reddit-keyword-monitoring-tools)
- **X advanced search:** a form at x.com/search-advanced, and operators can also be typed in the search bar. `min_faves:N` filters by likes (web search only; the API uses `min_likes:`). `since:` is inclusive and `until:` exclusive, in UTC. Example: `from:account "keyword" since:2025-01-01 min_faves:1000 -filter:replies`. Results require login (since Jul 2023). — [Typefully](https://typefully.com/blog/twitter-x-advanced-search); [AutoTweet, 2026](https://www.autotweet.io/blog/x-advanced-search-operators-2026); [X API operator docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/posts/search/integrate/operators)
- One source notes that `min_faves:`, `min_retweets:` and `min_replies:` aren't on X's help pages, so their official status is unconfirmed. — [Circleboom](https://circleboom.com/blog/x-twitter-advanced-search-operators-the-complete-guide/)
- Reddit-sourced traffic and community threads are an established channel for gaming sites. — [NitroPay blog](https://blog.nitropay.com/the-reddit-traffic-playbook-turning-gaming-community-threads-into-a-durable-traffic-channel/)

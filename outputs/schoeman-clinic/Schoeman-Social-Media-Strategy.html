THE SCHOEMAN CLINIC

Social Media Growth Strategy

Turning attention into patients, built on your own numbers.
Prepared by WILBA for Dr Gina Schoeman · August 2026 · Phase 1: Social Growth
The one thing to know. The clinic does not have a content problem, and it does not need more followers. It needs two things it does not yet have: consistent reach to the right people, and a way to turn that reach into patients. This strategy fixes both, and it is built around the single biggest asset you now have: a faculty of exceptional doctors, each with a story worth following.

SECTION 2What the numbers are actually telling us
  1. June was a paid boost, not organic growth. You have told us the June spike came from boosting a post. A boost buys temporary reach for as long as it runs; when it stops, reach falls away, often below where it began, because the boost tends to attract people who do not go on to engage. That is exactly the shape your data shows. It is a common and completely fixable pattern.
  2. Followers are not the same as reach. Right now only about 1 in 100 of your followers see a given post. The number to grow is not followers; it is reach to the right people, and what they do next.
  3. The reach you did have led nowhere. In June the two accounts drew tens of thousands of views. Because there is currently no way to capture an interested viewer (no guide, no assessment, no email sign-up), that attention simply passed through. This is the single biggest opportunity in this document.
  4. Two accounts are splitting the effort. People follow people. Dr Gina's account is already the larger of the two.

SECTION 4The strategy in one page

Stop chasing followers. Build reach to the right people, then capture and nurture them until they are ready to book.

The path a patient takes

Attention (short video and educational posts from the faculty) → a reason to raise their hand (a simple, useful self-assessment or guide) → their email → a warm nurture sequence → a free discovery call → a consultation → a patient who refers others.

Everything on social has one job: to feed the top of that path. Today the path has no on-ramp; the first fix is to build one.

The four moves

  1. Lead with people. Consolidate around Dr Gina and the faculty as the faces, feeding the clinic brand.
  2. Build the on-ramp. Add one simple lead capture (for example a short, educational self-assessment) so interested viewers become a list you own, not attention that disappears.
  3. Turn the faculty into a content engine. A light, repeatable capture process that respects everyone's time (detailed below).
  4. Measure what matters. Track reach to the right people, saves and shares, sign-ups, and discovery calls booked, not vanity follower counts.
SECTION 5Who we are speaking to

Your own patient data points to two people, and everything we publish is aimed at them, which also gently filters out those who are not a fit.

  • The patient who has been dismissed. Typically a woman in her late forties to sixties, told her results are "normal" while she still feels unwell, who has seen specialist after specialist. She wants to be understood and taken seriously.
  • The proactive investor in their health. Forties to fifties, wants advanced diagnostics and to get ahead of problems before they appear.

Both are evidence-led and sceptical of wellness fluff. The content wins by being genuinely useful and rigorous, which is exactly the clinic's natural voice.

SECTION 6The content engine

Content territories (each doctor owns one)

  • Why "normal" results miss the picture (Dr Gina) — the root-cause thesis.
  • Gut health and how it shapes everything (Dr Nikita, Dr Zia).
  • Hormones and midlife (Dr Gina, Dr Sonia).
  • Longevity and getting ahead of disease (Dr Nathan).

How we produce it without draining the doctors

One monthly capture session: each doctor answers a handful of real patient questions to camera, or records short voice notes. From that raw material we produce a month of content, reshaped for each platform. This is the difference between a plan that survives contact with busy clinicians and one that does not.

Formats: short educational video (the reach driver), simple carousels people save, and Dr Gina's story and philosophy woven throughout so the brand stays human.

SECTION 8The on-ramp: turning reach into patients

This is the piece that is missing today. We add a single, low-pressure entry point that gives real value and captures an email, for example a short educational self-assessment that helps someone understand their own symptoms and points them toward a discovery call. From there, a warm email sequence keeps in touch until they are ready.

Compliance is built in. This is regulated healthcare, so the on-ramp and the emails are designed to the correct consent standard, and the patient consent and privacy work is already prepared and with your data team. No claims, no diagnosis, education only.

SECTION 9Borrowed audiences and referrals

The fastest reach is not built from zero; it is borrowed from audiences that already trust someone. The clinic is well placed here: the faculty's own profiles and press, your existing referral network of specialists, and relevant podcasts and communities. Alongside this, a simple patient referral approach built on recognition and access, which suits this patient base far better than discounts.

SECTION 10How we will know it is working

We report on outcomes, not vanity. Every month:

  • Reach to the right audience, and saves and shares (the signals that content is landing).
  • Email sign-ups from the on-ramp (the list you own).
  • Discovery calls booked, and consultations that follow.

The system also remembers what works and what does not, so each month's content is sharper than the last. This is what makes the approach evidence-led rather than guesswork.

SECTION 11The first 90 days

Consultations and bloods

Consultations: concise 45 min 350, standard 60 min 500, comprehensive 90 min 750. Follow-ups: 30 min 250, 15 min brief 125, 45 min 350.

Bloods: mandatory before the first consultation, via TDL (Wimpole Street), home kit, or own results. Menstruating patients test days 18 to 22. Consultation about one month after the test.

Blood panels and prices: received (GS1 to GS13). A couple of quick confirms below.

Appointments, recall and letters

Cancellations: 2 working days notice or the full fee applies.

Recall: reminder for a planned follow-up 6 weeks ahead. Re-engagement flow for overdue patients, who are also given to the admin team as a list and an email.

Appointment reminders: handled by Semble, so we do not duplicate them.

Post-consultation letter: ready within 5 working days, with agreed holding responses.

Clinical safety, so far

Results: always go to the clinician, never sent to the patient, unless the patient supplies their own.

Routing: all clinical questions reviewed by a clinician; appointment date changes always handled by a PA.

Tone, coverage and out of hours

Tone: warm, patient centred and polished; no negative wording; no emojis; no comparisons to other clinics; sign off "Best wishes, The Schoeman Clinic Team".

Coverage: shared PA inbox, office hours Monday to Friday 9 to 5. Agreed out-of-hours and escalation holding messages in place.

PART 1, THE PRIORITYThe escalation boundary

The framework is agreed: operational stays with the team, anything clinical goes to a clinician, urgent gets an immediate human. These are the last details to make it watertight, in your words.

  1. Medication questions. The exact holding line a patient receives for "should I change my dose", "should I stop my medication", "is this side effect normal", while a clinician takes over.
  2. Safeguarding and crisis. A mental health crisis, self harm, or an emergency. What is the patient told immediately (999 / 111), which words or phrases must always trigger an urgent flag, and who is notified, and how.
  3. Sounds admin, is clinical. Two or three real examples ("can I take X with Y", "is this safe in pregnancy") so the line is unmistakable.
  4. Never answer. The short list of anything the assistant must never answer, under any circumstances.
  5. Repeat prescriptions. Exactly what the team checks before approving a repeat (the treatment plan), and what to do if the patient is overdue for a follow-up.

The case

The pilot already told us where the money works

We ran Google Search and Facebook side by side for the pilot. Facebook won, and it wasn't close. It brought in more real redemptions at roughly a third of the cost.

What we measuredGoogle SearchFacebook and Instagram
Spend (lifetime)$2,957 gross$1,071
Cost per lead~$20$3.59
Redemptions it drove1421
Cost per redemption~$147~$51

Google's conversion tracking hit a form bug during the pilot, so I treat its conversion figures as directional. The redemptions above come straight from GHL, and they're the number that matters.

Facebook drove more redemptions at a third of the cost. That's the whole argument.
Two more reasons

Google can't do the two things we most want

Parties are priced out of Search

"Birthday parties Orlando" runs $25 to $40 a click on Google. That's unworkable. On Facebook I reach the exact parent audience for about $3 a lead, and parties are the highest value thing Monkey Joe's sells.

Retargeting has barely run

The warmest audience, people who already visited, watched, or joined the list, has had almost no spend behind it. It's the cheapest place to win and it's sitting switched off.

The strategy

Two offers up top, a retargeting engine underneath

Top of funnel
50% offOur best offer. Cold parents, drives weekday walk ins.
Top of funnel
Birthdays$25 off a party. Never advertised before now.
The engine
RetargetingEveryone who engaged, hit again with both offers.
Result
Redeem and bookWalk ins in store, parties booked with the code.

Cold ads for the two offers fill the audience. Retargeting then converts the people who leaned in. Every redemption ties back to Facebook because the tracking already carries the source into GHL. We proved that in the pilot.

The build

A clear roadmap, live inside two weeks

This is not a slow build. The offers are proven and the tracking already works, so we launch fast and let the retargeting engine compound from there.

Phase 1Week 1
FoundationsConfirm the Pixel and Conversions API are firing on the offer, redemption and party pages. Build the audiences. Lock the UTMs so every redemption ties back to Facebook in GHL. First creative ready for both offers.
Phase 2Week 2
Launch the offers50% off and birthday prospecting go live at both stores. Daily eyes on it while the campaigns learn, budgets held steady.
Phase 3Weeks 3 to 4
Turn on retargetingThe warm audience engine goes live. First optimisation pass: cut what is not working, push budget to what is. First weekly scorecard lands in your inbox.
Phase 4Month 2
Optimise and scaleLookalike audiences built off the people who redeemed. Creative refresh and offer testing. Budget weighted to the stronger store.
Phase 5Month 3
Parties and reviewFull birthday push into the summer, enquiries handed to the front desk with deposits. A 90 day results pack and a clear recommendation for what comes next.

The bottom line

Spend the same money on the channel that actually delivers

The pilot proved the model. $4,027 in spend turned into 479 codes and 101 in store redemptions, and Facebook did the heavy lifting per dollar. The two biggest levers, retargeting and parties, are still switched off. Turn them on, on Facebook, and this compounds.

One channel. Two offers. Retargeting doing the work underneath. I'd like to build it.
